the practice

000

Specialists in security. Students of organizations.

Six Ravens is an independent security research and advisory practice specializing in detection and response, incident investigations and organizational preparedness. We examine how these capabilities work in practice, bringing together technical evidence and the people, decisions and institutional conditions that shape their effectiveness.

Our work also encompasses third-party relationships and security culture. We investigate how outside organizations influence a company's security capabilities, and help security teams build the identity, trust and shared purpose that make people more effective together.

Security programs reflect years of decisions about architecture, staffing, investments, leadership, outside providers and the way people work together. Those decisions shape not only what an organization can do, but what it believes about its ability to do it.

the central idea

Understanding is part of security.

We look beyond whether technology works as designed to how organizations come to understand, depend upon and adapt to it.

what we do

Our deepest specialization is detection and response. The same attention to technical, human and institutional conditions runs through everything we do.

01 deepest specialization

Detection
+ response.

Understand what your program can recognize, what it can do about it, and why.

Detection and response is where years of security decisions become consequential. Architecture, intelligence, staffing, governance and the choices made about tools and processes shape what an organization recognizes and how it acts.

We investigate how technical capabilities, human judgment, incentives and institutional structures influence outcomes. A detection rule can work exactly as written while the program around it produces an unexpected result.

how we think

002

Behavior isn't a component.
It's what the system produces.

A security outcome doesn't reside in a tool, an analyst, a policy or a vendor. It emerges from what happens between them.

Some complexity is inherent. Some is inherited. Some is created by choices nobody remembers making.

We can't simplify away the mathematics inside a model, the architecture of a distributed system or the technical facts of an incident. Those demand genuine engineering knowledge.

But the arrangements around those technologies are different. Who sees the evidence? Who can question an automated interpretation? What happens when one team needs another to act? Which metrics shape the choices people make? Those conditions develop through people and institutions, and they can be investigated.

Our work combines technical investigation with attention to culture, incentives, authority, history and organizational knowledge. The objective isn't to identify a single broken component. It's to understand the behavior the whole system produces, including what works well and why.

AI makes this more consequential

Security tools and business systems increasingly interpret evidence, exercise permissions and make decisions. Our questions about trust, authority, independent evidence and organizational response apply to these systems, too.

when we're useful

003

There's a reason you called.

Not every engagement begins because something went wrong. Often the organization has a consequential question that deserves an independent examination or an experienced outside perspective.

01 a new leader

You inherited the security program.

You weren't there for the decisions that built it. The metrics, architecture, priorities and operating assumptions come with their own history.

Establish what deserves confidence, what has changed, and which decisions matter now.

02 an incident or near miss

The immediate incident is over. The questions aren't.

The technical sequence may be clear while the organizational circumstances that made it possible remain poorly understood.

Get an evidence-based reconstruction that accounts for what people knew at the time.

03 dependencies

You've delegated the work. Not the consequences.

Outside providers and connected systems make decisions that affect the organization, often beyond its direct control.

Understand the dependencies, authority and expectations that determine what happens when they matter.

04 culture + capability

A capable team can become a community.

Teams develop their own habits and identity whether anyone intends it or not. Those qualities affect trust, judgment and collaboration.

Build a shared identity and experiences the people involved can sustain themselves.

how an engagement runs

004

Serious work. Plain arrangements.

  1. Begin with the question.

    Tell us what prompted the call. We ask about the people, capabilities and circumstances involved, and agree on a focused question, a scope and an end to the work.

  2. Work alongside your people.

    They bring knowledge no outside investigator can replace. Together we examine evidence, understand decisions and challenge interpretations grounded in the organization's circumstances.

  3. Leave something useful.

    An independent reconstruction, exercise decision history, analysis of dependencies, or a team identity and traditions its members helped create. The outcome is suited to the engagement, not a predetermined report template.

The fine print

Six Ravens undertakes focused investigative and advisory engagements. We don't operate a SOC, provide emergency incident response or assume operational responsibility for client systems.

on the name

007

Six Ravens.

For centuries, ravens have been associated with the Tower of London. According to legend, six ravens must remain there; should they ever leave, the Tower will fall, and the kingdom with it.

The origins of the legend are less certain than its telling. The ravens remain.

We know better than to mistake a good story for evidence. We also know better than to underestimate the power of one.
